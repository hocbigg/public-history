Curate your own digital exhibits

  • Creating an Omeka Exhibit (The Programming Historian) - A peer-reviewed lesson detailing how to structure narratives, select metadata themes, and arrange digital items into guided online exhibits.
  • Building a Digital Exhibition with CollectionBuilder and the Internet Archive (The Programming Historian) - A step-by-step walkthrough for building a static digital exhibition site using GitHub Pages, Markdown, and hosted digital collections.
  • CollectionBuilder-GH Walkthrough (CollectionBuilder) - A hands-on guide for generating a free, searchable digital collection and exhibition website using Google Sheets and GitHub.

Build your own interactive timelines

  • TimelineJS User Guide and Spreadsheet Template (Northwestern University Knight Lab) - The official authoring walkthrough and Google Sheets template for building interactive, media-rich web timelines.
  • Making a Timeline from a Google Spreadsheet (Knight Lab Documentation) - Technical guide covering date formatting conventions (including BCE dates), media embedding syntax, and background customization.

Build your own narrative story maps

  • StoryMapJS Authoring Tool and Walkthrough (Northwestern University Knight Lab) - The official platform guide for linking historical events to geographic coordinates and multimedia slides.
  • Displaying a Georeferenced Map in Knight Lab's StoryMapJS (The Programming Historian) - A step-by-step tutorial on taking historic map rasters from Map Warper and displaying them as custom baselayers in StoryMapJS.

Conduct your own oral history interviews

  • A Practical Guide to Oral History (Southern Oral History Program) - A complete field manual covering interview preparation, question drafting, ethical consent forms, and remote recording setups.
  • How to Do Oral History (Smithsonian Institution Archives) - An institutional guide covering the entire workflow from pre-interview biographical research to sound recording and indexing.
  • Principles and Best Practices for Oral History (Oral History Association) - The professional association's official ethical benchmarks and methodological standards for interviewers and narrators.

Design your own historical walking tours

  • Stepping into the Past with Historical Walking Tours (Active History) - A practitioner's step-by-step walkthrough covering route design, stop pacing, archival research integration, and public delivery.
  • How to Create a Walking Tour or Heritage Trail in Clio (Clio) - The official instructional guide for connecting geolocated historic landmarks into an interactive public walking or driving tour.

Produce your own history podcast episodes

  • How to Make an Oral History Podcast (Historica Canada) - A structured educational guide detailing how to transform recorded interviews into scripted narrative audio episodes with narration and archival audio.
  • The Audio Project Checklist (Transom) - Professional guidance on microphone technique, scripting for the ear, rough-cut assembly, and narrative audio editing workflows; search "The Audio Project Checklist Transom" to access the guide directly.

Launch your own crowdsourced transcription projects

  • How to Create a Project with our Project Builder (Zooniverse Help Center) - A complete technical walkthrough covering project configuration, workflow creation, subject upload, and volunteer task design.
  • Lifecycle of a Crowdsourced Transcription Project (Zooniverse Help Center) - Specialized guidance on formatting handwritten documents, designing transcription task interfaces, and validating public contributions.

Compile your own archival finding aids

  • Describing Archives: A Content Standard (Society of American Archivists) - The official, open-access national standard defining the data elements and rules for constructing collection-level and item-level finding aids.
  • Processing Manual for Archival and Manuscript Collections (UMass Boston Special Collections) - An open, practical repository manual providing step-by-step processing workflows, series arrangement guidance, and an archival finding aid template.

Prepare your own historic place nominations

  • How to Complete the National Register Registration Form (National Register Bulletin 16A / National Park Service) - The definitive federal technical manual detailing how to research historic context, delineate property boundaries, establish physical integrity, and complete NPS Form 10-900.
  • How to Apply the National Register Criteria for Evaluation (National Register Bulletin 15 / National Park Service) - The companion federal guide for evaluating property significance across events, persons, architecture, and archaeological potential.

Author your own historical marker applications

  • Create Your Own Georgia Historical Marker Guide and Documentation Worksheet (Georgia Historical Society) - A guided research methodology and documentation template for writing concise, fact-checked, 130-word public roadside marker inscriptions.
  • Apply for a Historical Marker (Texas Historical Commission) - Official state guidelines and application packet outlining requirements for historical narrative essays, archival source citations, and site boundary verification.

Write your own history op-eds

  • Op-Ed Writing: The Basics (The OpEd Project) - The premier open resource for structuring opinion pieces, identifying news hooks and ledes, marshaling historical evidence, and pitching to editors.
  • On the Power of Op-Eds (American Historical Association) - An essay and practitioner guide on how professional historians translate archival findings and historiographical arguments into high-impact public essays.

Curate your own physical museum exhibits

  • A Guide to Exhibit Development (Smithsonian Exhibits) - The Smithsonian's internal manual detailing the exhibition lifecycle: developing the interpretive hierarchy, writing 3-tier object labels, designing visitor flow, and managing team milestones.
  • Exhibit Design and Development Workbook (Texas Historical Commission) - A 53-page workbook for small museums and historical societies covering theme formulation, storyline development, visual selection, and physical fabrication on modest budgets.

Write your own public humanities grant proposals

  • Public Humanities Projects Notice of Funding Opportunity and Application Instructions (National Endowment for the Humanities) - The official grant guidelines and narrative criteria for securing planning and implementation funding for historic places, exhibits, and public programs.
  • How to Get a Grant from NEH (National Endowment for the Humanities) - Agency-published insider advice detailing how to articulate project significance, define humanities themes, structure project work plans, and avoid common review pitfalls.
