Hocbigg - Public History

Path to a free self-taught education in Public History.

Last updated on: 2026-10-08

Introduction

Public history is the practice of preserving, interpreting, and presenting the past in collaboration with and for the public. While traditional academic history primarily communicates historical scholarship through peer-reviewed monographs and scholarly journals, public history operates in civic and community spaces—including museums, historic landmarks, community archives, national parks, and digital media. At the center of the discipline is the concept of shared authority: the idea that professional historians and community members collaborate as partners to interpret lived experiences, artifacts, and place-based narratives.

This curriculum is built for independent, self-directed learners coming to the field with no prior formal training in history, archival science, or museum studies. It assumes no institutional affiliation or academic prerequisites. Rather than focusing on abstract academic research mechanics or thesis writing, this curriculum emphasizes the foundational concepts, ethical guidelines, and practical workflows needed to understand how history is mediated in public life.

How to Navigate the Curriculum

The curriculum is structured into seven core subjects, divided between conceptual foundations and distinct operational areas:

  • Start with the foundations: Begin with Introduction to Public History and Public Memory and Heritage Studies. These two areas establish the essential vocabulary of the discipline, examine the ethics of public engagement, and explain how societies construct, commemorate, and contest collective narratives.
  • Explore the operational pillars in any order: The remaining five subjects—Museum Studies and Interpretation, Historic Preservation and Cultural Resource Management, Oral History, Archives and Records Management, and Digital Public History—represent the primary arenas where public historians do their work. Because these five practical domains do not strictly depend on one another, you can study them in sequence or prioritize the specific medium that matches your personal interests.

This page is strictly a foundational core curriculum. It covers only the baseline knowledge, standards, and methodologies that every practitioner in the field should understand before branching out into specialized subfields. Once you have completed these core subjects, you can branch out into specialized subfields in Advanced Topics.

Curriculum

Introduction to Public History

The foundational survey of what public history is, the distinction between academic and public history, the ethics of shared authority, and the diverse environments where public historians engage communities with the past.

Applied Public History: Places, People, Stories (Coursera / University of London) - A free-to-audit online course offering an interactive entry point into place-based research, community storytelling, and practical public history tools.

Introduction to Public History: Interpreting the Past, Engaging Audiences (Cherstin M. Lyon, Elizabeth M. Nix, and Rebecca K. Shrum / Rowman & Littlefield) - A concise introductory textbook that serves as a structured foundational spine, exploring the core questions of collecting, interpreting, and engaging audiences.

Public History: A Textbook of Practice (Thomas Cauvin / Routledge) - An alternative to Lyon et al., this comprehensive textbook explores public history practice across international contexts, media productions, and civic consulting in greater operational detail.

Public Memory and Heritage Studies

The theoretical study of how societies construct, institutionalize, and contest historical narratives, and how collective memory informs public commemoration and heritage preservation.

The Collective Memory (Maurice Halbwachs, trans. Mary Douglas) - The foundational theoretical text establishing how memory functions as an active social construct rather than an individual psychological faculty; available to borrow freely on Internet Archive.

The Past Is a Foreign Country — Revisited (David Lowenthal / Cambridge University Press) - Builds on foundational memory theory to examine how modern societies selectively preserve, reshape, and commercialize the past through heritage and nostalgia.

The Collective Memory Reader (Jeffrey K. Olick, Vered Vinitzky-Seroussi, and Daniel Levy, eds. / Oxford University Press) - A complementary anthology of seminal primary and secondary readings that broadens both Halbwachs and Lowenthal across political, cultural, and psychological dimensions of collective memory.

Museum Studies and Interpretation

The concepts and methods behind artifact curation, visitor engagement, and exhibition design in museums, historic houses, and cultural institutions.

Interpreting Our Heritage (Freeman Tilden / University of North Carolina Press) - The seminal philosophical work establishing the core principles of heritage interpretation, emphasizing audience connection and holistic storytelling; free to borrow on Internet Archive.

Foundations of Interpretation (Eppley Center for Parks and Public Lands / Indiana University) - Translates Tilden's philosophy into practical visitor communication skills, interpretive program design, and audience analysis in a free, self-paced format.

Tangible Things: Discovering History Through Artworks, Artifacts, Scientific Specimens, and the Stuff Around You (HarvardX / edX) - A complementary online course focusing specifically on material culture and artifact analysis, demonstrating how physical objects are curated to create museum narratives.

Creating Meaningful and Inclusive Museum Practices (International Council of Museums / FutureLearn) - Builds on basic museum concepts by examining participatory curation and methods for collaborating ethically with historically marginalized communities.

Historic Preservation and Cultural Resource Management

The philosophies, legal structures, documentation methods, and community strategies used to identify, protect, and interpret historic buildings and cultural landscapes.

Historic Preservation: An Introduction to Its History, Principles, and Practice (Norman Tyler, Ted J. Ligibel, and Ilene R. Tyler / W. W. Norton) - The standard introductory textbook covering preservation philosophy, architectural survey techniques, historic district designation, and legal frameworks.

History and Theory of Historic Preservation (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A complementary university course providing lecture notes, syllabi, and readings that examine the political debates and public policies shaping preservation decisions.

Oral History

The methodology, ethical frameworks, legal standards, and recording practices used to collect, preserve, and interpret first-person spoken testimonies.

Doing Oral History (Donald A. Ritchie / Oxford University Press) - The definitive practical manual covering project planning, ethical interviewing techniques, legal release forms, and public presentation.

Introduction to Oral History (Baylor University Institute for Oral History) - A free, step-by-step practical manual that serves as an accessible starting guide to project setup, question design, and recording workflows; search "Introduction to Oral History Baylor PDF" to access the guide directly.

The Oral History Reader (Robert Perks and Alistair Thomson, eds. / Routledge) - A complementary anthology of international essays that deepens Ritchie's practical instruction with theoretical discussions on memory, subjectivity, and community empowerment.

Archives and Records Management

The fundamental theories and practical techniques for identifying, appraising, arranging, describing, preserving, and providing public access to records and manuscript collections.

Understanding Archives & Manuscripts (James M. O'Toole and Richard J. Cox / Society of American Archivists) - The foundational conceptual textbook exploring the nature of archives, core archival values, and the principles of provenance and original order.

Developing and Maintaining Practical Archives: A How-To-Do-It Manual (Gregory S. Hunter / Neal-Schuman) - Builds on O'Toole and Cox with comprehensive, hands-on instructions for appraisal, accessioning, arrangement, descriptive finding aids, and preservation management.

Records Management Training Online Lessons (U.S. National Archives and Records Administration) - A complementary suite of free, self-paced government modules that provide practical training in records schedules, electronic records, and regulatory compliance.

Digital Public History

The application of digital technologies, open-source platforms, and web-authoring practices to preserve historical artifacts and engage online communities.

Digital History: A Guide to Gathering, Preserving, and Presenting the Past on the Web (Daniel J. Cohen and Roy Rosenzweig / University of Pennsylvania Press) - The foundational open-access textbook outlining the principles of digitization, copyright, digital preservation, and designing public historical websites.

The Programming Historian (ProgHist Ltd) - A complementary open-access platform of peer-reviewed, step-by-step lessons teaching practical digital skills, including web mapping and digital exhibit construction, to use alongside Cohen and Rosenzweig.
