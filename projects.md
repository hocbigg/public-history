## Curate your own digital exhibits

- [Creating an Omeka Exhibit (The Programming Historian)](https://programminghistorian.org/en/lessons/creating-an-omeka-exhibit) - A peer-reviewed lesson detailing how to structure narratives, select metadata themes, and arrange digital items into guided online exhibits.
- [Building a Digital Exhibition with CollectionBuilder and the Internet Archive (The Programming Historian)](https://programminghistorian.org/en/lessons/building-a-digital-exhibition-with-collectionbuilder-and-the-internet-archive) - A step-by-step walkthrough for building a static digital exhibition site using GitHub Pages, Markdown, and hosted digital collections.
- [CollectionBuilder-GH Walkthrough (CollectionBuilder)](https://collectionbuilder.github.io/cb-docs/docs/walkthroughs/gh-walkthrough/) - A hands-on guide for generating a free, searchable digital collection and exhibition website using Google Sheets and GitHub.

## Build your own interactive timelines

- [TimelineJS User Guide and Spreadsheet Template (Northwestern University Knight Lab)](https://timeline.knightlab.com/) - The official authoring walkthrough and Google Sheets template for building interactive, media-rich web timelines.
- [Making a Timeline from a Google Spreadsheet (Knight Lab Documentation)](https://timeline.knightlab.com/docs/using-spreadsheets.html) - Technical guide covering date formatting conventions (including BCE dates), media embedding syntax, and background customization.

## Build your own narrative story maps

- [StoryMapJS Authoring Tool and Walkthrough (Northwestern University Knight Lab)](https://storymap.knightlab.com/) - The official platform guide for linking historical events to geographic coordinates and multimedia slides.
- [Displaying a Georeferenced Map in Knight Lab's StoryMapJS (The Programming Historian)](https://programminghistorian.org/en/lessons/georeferenced-maps-storymap-js) - A step-by-step tutorial on taking historic map rasters from Map Warper and displaying them as custom baselayers in StoryMapJS.

## Conduct your own oral history interviews

- [A Practical Guide to Oral History (Southern Oral History Program)](https://sohp.org/resources-2/our-resources/) - A complete field manual covering interview preparation, question drafting, ethical consent forms, and remote recording setups.
- [How to Do Oral History (Smithsonian Institution Archives)](https://siarchives.si.edu/history/oral-history/how-to-do-oral-history) - An institutional guide covering the entire workflow from pre-interview biographical research to sound recording and indexing.
- [Principles and Best Practices for Oral History (Oral History Association)](https://oralhistory.org/best-practices/) - The professional association's official ethical benchmarks and methodological standards for interviewers and narrators.

## Design your own historical walking tours

- [Stepping into the Past with Historical Walking Tours (Active History)](https://activehistory.ca/2013/10/stepping-into-the-past-with-historical-walking-tours/) - A practitioner's step-by-step walkthrough covering route design, stop pacing, archival research integration, and public delivery.
- [How to Create a Walking Tour or Heritage Trail in Clio (Clio)](https://theclio.com/about) - The official instructional guide for connecting geolocated historic landmarks into an interactive public walking or driving tour.

## Produce your own history podcast episodes

- [How to Make an Oral History Podcast (Historica Canada)](https://www.historicacanada.ca/education-guides/how-to-make-an-oral-history-podcast) - A structured educational guide detailing how to transform recorded interviews into scripted narrative audio episodes with narration and archival audio.
- [The Audio Project Checklist (Transom)](https://transom.org/) - Professional guidance on microphone technique, scripting for the ear, rough-cut assembly, and narrative audio editing workflows; search "The Audio Project Checklist Transom" to access the guide directly.

## Launch your own crowdsourced transcription projects

- [How to Create a Project with our Project Builder (Zooniverse Help Center)](https://help.zooniverse.org/getting-started/) - A complete technical walkthrough covering project configuration, workflow creation, subject upload, and volunteer task design.
- [Lifecycle of a Crowdsourced Transcription Project (Zooniverse Help Center)](https://help.zooniverse.org/getting-started/transcription/) - Specialized guidance on formatting handwritten documents, designing transcription task interfaces, and validating public contributions.

## Compile your own archival finding aids

- [Describing Archives: A Content Standard (Society of American Archivists)](https://www2.archivists.org/standards/DACS) - The official, open-access national standard defining the data elements and rules for constructing collection-level and item-level finding aids.
- [Processing Manual for Archival and Manuscript Collections (UMass Boston Special Collections)](https://scholarworks.umb.edu/uasc_manual/3/) - An open, practical repository manual providing step-by-step processing workflows, series arrangement guidance, and an archival finding aid template.

## Prepare your own historic place nominations

- [How to Complete the National Register Registration Form (National Register Bulletin 16A / National Park Service)](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/upload/NRB16A-Complete.pdf) - The definitive federal technical manual detailing how to research historic context, delineate property boundaries, establish physical integrity, and complete NPS Form 10-900.
- [How to Apply the National Register Criteria for Evaluation (National Register Bulletin 15 / National Park Service)](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/upload/NRB15_508.pdf) - The companion federal guide for evaluating property significance across events, persons, architecture, and archaeological potential.

## Author your own historical marker applications

- [Create Your Own Georgia Historical Marker Guide and Documentation Worksheet (Georgia Historical Society)](https://georgiahistory.com/education-outreach/historical-marker-program/create-your-own-historical-marker/) - A guided research methodology and documentation template for writing concise, fact-checked, 130-word public roadside marker inscriptions.
- [Apply for a Historical Marker (Texas Historical Commission)](https://www.thc.texas.gov/preserve/projects-and-programs/state-historical-markers/apply-historical-marker) - Official state guidelines and application packet outlining requirements for historical narrative essays, archival source citations, and site boundary verification.

## Write your own history op-eds

- [Op-Ed Writing: The Basics (The OpEd Project)](https://www.theopedproject.org/resources) - The premier open resource for structuring opinion pieces, identifying news hooks and ledes, marshaling historical evidence, and pitching to editors.
- [On the Power of Op-Eds (American Historical Association)](https://www.historians.org/research-and-publications/perspectives-on-history/january-2025/on-the-power-of-op-eds) - An essay and practitioner guide on how professional historians translate archival findings and historiographical arguments into high-impact public essays.

## Curate your own physical museum exhibits

- [A Guide to Exhibit Development (Smithsonian Exhibits)](https://exhibits.si.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Guide-to-Exhibit-Development.pdf) - The Smithsonian's internal manual detailing the exhibition lifecycle: developing the interpretive hierarchy, writing 3-tier object labels, designing visitor flow, and managing team milestones.
- [Exhibit Design and Development Workbook (Texas Historical Commission)](https://www.thc.texas.gov/public/upload/publications/2015%20LR%20Museum%20Services%20Exhibit%20Development%20Workbook%20with%20Introduction.pdf) - A 53-page workbook for small museums and historical societies covering theme formulation, storyline development, visual selection, and physical fabrication on modest budgets.

## Write your own public humanities grant proposals

- [Public Humanities Projects Notice of Funding Opportunity and Application Instructions (National Endowment for the Humanities)](https://www.neh.gov/grants/public/public-humanities-projects) - The official grant guidelines and narrative criteria for securing planning and implementation funding for historic places, exhibits, and public programs.
- [How to Get a Grant from NEH (National Endowment for the Humanities)](https://www.neh.gov/humanities/2008/julyaugust/feature/how-get-grant-neh) - Agency-published insider advice detailing how to articulate project significance, define humanities themes, structure project work plans, and avoid common review pitfalls.
