Public History — Advanced Topics

Track Index

Choose one or two specialization tracks aligned with your professional and research interests; advanced learners are not expected to complete every track.

  • Digital Public History & Spatial Humanities — Advanced methodologies for spatial analysis, historical GIS, web exhibit curation, born-digital preservation, and crowdsourced public participation.
  • Museum Curation and Exhibition Practice — Advanced operational frameworks for exhibition storytelling, preventive conservation, audience research, and Indigenous decolonizing methodologies.
  • Difficult Heritage, Transitional Justice & Memory Politics — Conceptual and practical frameworks for interpreting atrocity, resolving civic conflicts over public monuments, and curating memorial sites.
  • Historic Preservation and Cultural Resource Management — Legal compliance, federal regulatory review (Section 106), building rehabilitation standards, and architectural documentation methodologies.
  • Community Engagement and Shared Authority — Methodologies for collaborative fieldwork, participatory action research, community co-curation, and digital oral history curation.

Digital Public History & Spatial Humanities

Advanced methods for building digital history projects, including spatial analysis and GIS mapping, web exhibit curation, born-digital archiving, and public crowdsourcing.

Spatial History and Historical GIS: The Spatial Humanities: GIS and the Future of Humanities Scholarship (David J. Bodenhamer, John Corrigan, and Trevor M. Harris, eds. / Indiana University Press) - A seminal volume exploring how spatial thinking, deep mapping, and geographic information systems reshape historical inquiry and public interpretation.

Spatial History and Historical GIS: Geocoding Historical Data Using QGIS (Justin Colson / The Programming Historian) - A practical, step-by-step open tutorial on translating historical tabular records into spatial coordinates and analyzing them in open-source GIS software.

Digital Exhibit Curation: Creating an Omeka Exhibit (Miriam Posner and Megan R. Brett / The Programming Historian) - A peer-reviewed, open tutorial guiding practitioners through building structured, narrative-driven online exhibitions using the premier open-source public history platform.

Born-Digital Preservation and Web Archiving: Web Archiving for Public Libraries (WebJunction / Internet Archive) - A free, self-paced training course developed with the Internet Archive covering workflows, selection policies, and preservation of local web content.

Crowdsourcing and Participatory Digital Projects: The Collective Wisdom Handbook: Perspectives on Crowdsourcing in Cultural Heritage (Mia Ridge et al. / British Library PubPub) - An authoritative open-access field guide offering practical methodologies, volunteer motivation frameworks, and case studies for designing ethical, community-powered crowdsourcing initiatives.

Museum Curation and Exhibition Practice

Specialized methodologies for developing exhibition narratives, managing and preserving collections, centering Indigenous perspectives, and studying visitor interactions.

Exhibition Concept and Narrative Development: Exhibit Labels: An Interpretive Approach (Beverly Serrell and Katherine Whitney / Rowman & Littlefield) - The definitive professional manual for formulating the "Big Idea," structuring exhibition hierarchies, and writing clear, visitor-centered labels.

Preventive Conservation and Collections Management: Museum Handbook, Part I: Museum Collections (U.S. National Park Service) - The premier open-access technical manual outlining standard operating procedures for preventive conservation, storage environments, integrated pest management, and collections planning.

Decolonizing Museology and Repatriation: Decolonizing Museums: Representing Native America in National and Tribal Museums (Amy Lonetree / University of North Carolina Press) - The seminal monograph analyzing how tribal and national museums navigate Indigenous collaboration, tribal sovereignty, and the repatriation of cultural belongings.

Decolonizing Museology and Repatriation: Amy Lonetree Keynote: Indigenizing Museums and the Move Toward Decolonization (Thinking Through the Museum / YouTube) - A recorded keynote lecture detailing the practical evolution of collaborative curation and structural tribal consultation in museum practice.

Visitor Studies and Audience Research: The Museum Experience Revisited (John H. Falk and Lynn D. Dierking / Routledge) - The landmark research monograph introducing the Contextual Model of Learning, examining how visitors construct meaning across personal, sociocultural, and physical dimensions.

Difficult Heritage, Transitional Justice & Memory Politics

Theoretical and practical approaches to interpreting painful, contested, and traumatic pasts through memorial museums, public monuments, and post-conflict truth processes.

Difficult Heritage and Perpetrator Commemoration: Difficult Heritage: Negotiating the Nazi Past in Nuremberg and Beyond (Sharon Macdonald / Routledge) - The foundational monograph analyzing how modern societies negotiate, curate, and acknowledge architectural and physical sites of atrocity and perpetration.

Contested Monuments and Public Memorialization: Monument(al) Challenges Toolkit (EuroClio & Contested Histories Initiative) - A free, practical toolkit and curriculum designed to analyze, teach, and resolve civic conflicts over contested statues, street names, and commemorative sites.

Memorial Museum Creation and Politics of Display: Preserving Memory: The Struggle to Create America's Holocaust Museum (Edward T. Linenthal / Internet Archive) - The definitive case study detailing the complex ideological, diplomatic, and architectural negotiations involved in establishing the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum; available to borrow freely on Internet Archive.

Post-Conflict Memorialization and Transitional Justice: From Memory to Action: A Toolkit for Memorialization in Post-Conflict Societies (Ereshnee Naidu / International Coalition of Sites of Conscience) - A practical, field-tested guide for memory practitioners facilitating dialogue, memorialization, and truth-telling in societies recovering from mass atrocities.

Historic Preservation and Cultural Resource Management

The legal frameworks, regulatory reviews, technical conservation standards, and documentation methods governing the protection and management of historic environments.

Federal Regulatory Compliance and Section 106: e-Learning Courses (Advisory Council on Historic Preservation) - Free official, on-demand modules covering the Section 106 review process, federal agency undertakings, adverse effect resolution, and tribal consultation.

Rehabilitation Standards and Treatment of Historic Properties: The Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation (National Park Service Technical Preservation Services) - The definitive federal guidelines and technical benchmarks governing repairs, alterations, and additions to historic buildings while preserving character-defining features.

Cultural Resource Management Practice and Law: Cultural Resource Laws and Practice (Thomas F. King / Rowman & Littlefield) - The standard industry reference for identifying, evaluating, and mitigating impacts on archaeological and historical resources under federal, state, and tribal laws.

Architectural Documentation and Field Survey: Standards and Guidelines for Architectural and Engineering Documentation (Heritage Documentation Programs / U.S. National Park Service) - The official technical criteria defining the production of measured drawings, large-format photographs, and written historical reports for the HABS, HAER, and HALS national collections.

Community Engagement and Shared Authority

Collaborative methodologies for sharing interpretive authority, empowering local community stakeholders, and capturing vernacular histories through digital and participatory workflows.

Shared Authority and Public History Theory: A Shared Authority: Essays on the Craft and Meaning of Oral and Public History (Michael Frisch / Internet Archive) - The foundational theoretical text establishing public history as an egalitarian, collaborative process between historical professionals and communities; available to borrow freely on Internet Archive.

Collaborative Curation and User Participation: Letting Go? Sharing Historical Authority in a User-Generated World (Bill Adair, Benjamin Filene, and Laura Koloski, eds. / Pew Center for Arts & Heritage) - Case studies and critical reflections by leading practitioners examining the challenges, ethical tensions, and practical strategies of relinquishing institutional control to public contributors.

Community-Based Participatory Action Research (CBPAR): A Short Guide to Community Based Participatory Action Research (Janice C. Burns, Deanna Y. Cooke, and Christine Schweidler / UCLA Labor Center) - A practical field manual detailing participatory research design, asset mapping, and methods for engaging community partners as co-researchers rather than subjects.

Digital Oral History Workflow and Archiving: Oral History in the Digital Age (Doug Boyd et al. / MATRIX: Michigan State University) - A comprehensive open-access resource portal featuring curated video interviews and micro-essays covering digital recording technologies, indexing with OHMS, preservation, and online dissemination.
